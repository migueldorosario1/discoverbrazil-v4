To mark its 14th anniversary, Skift has published a sharp analysis of the travel industry's real power dynamics. The piece, titled 'Don't Start a Travel Startup,' distills 14 lessons from watching travel companies launch, grow, pivot, and fail. Its core argument: founders should not build generically 'in travel' but instead identify where power actually sits and target a structural struggle that incumbents cannot easily resolve.

The article includes a vintage travel-poster-style map that illustrates the full journey from a traveler's initial question to a booked room, seat, or tour. The map highlights the companies competing to control five key battlegrounds: discovery, booking, the customer record, inventory movement, and the traveler's wallet. Each of these areas is dominated by different players, making the value chain a complex web of competing interests.

According to Skift, no company currently owns a complete, portable memory of traveler preferences. Banks know spending, platforms know purchases, and suppliers know what happened on-property, but no single player holds the full picture of a traveler's preferences and intentions. This memory gap is framed as the key open opportunity in the industry.

The piece argues that this gap will become a major competitive battleground as AI agents take over more of search, booking, and servicing. With AI poised to handle these functions, the ability to 'remember' the traveler across the entire journey will be crucial. Skift promises a broader framework on this topic in the coming weeks.

For travelers, this analysis offers a glimpse into the forces shaping how we plan and book trips. The competition to own the traveler's memory could lead to more personalized experiences, but it also raises questions about data privacy and control. As the industry evolves, the traveler's wallet and preferences remain the ultimate prize.

Skift's map is a valuable tool for understanding the current landscape of travel technology and the strategic battles that will define its future. Whether you're a founder, investor, or curious traveler, the piece provides a clear-eyed view of where power lies and where the next big opportunity might emerge.

As the travel industry continues to evolve, the question of who truly 'remembers' the traveler will become increasingly central. Skift's analysis suggests that the answer will shape the next generation of travel startups and the experiences they offer. For now, the map serves as a reminder that in travel, power is not monolithic—it is distributed across a complex chain of players, each vying for control.

The full article is available on Skift's website, offering a detailed look at the 14 lessons and the power-struggle map. For those interested in the business of travel, it is a must-read.
