Amazon is betting that stored payment credentials, new MCP support, and 180 million Prime members can solve the problem that stopped OpenAI cold: getting travelers to complete a purchase inside an AI conversation.

According to Skift, Amazon's adoption of the Model Context Protocol for Alexa+ last week revealed several travel companies stepping up to be among the first partners.

Priceline, Viator, Virgin Atlantic, Lyft, Brightline, FlixBus, and Greyhound will build travel and transportation booking tools for Alexa+ later this year, the tech company said. Others will be able to connect their own MCP servers through the Alexa+ for Builders platform. A new wallet offering would also allow travelers to pay for trips using stored payment cards.

For travel companies, the open questions are whether Amazon Wallet becomes a new booking channel, and who owns the traveler data it generates.

The move arrives as rival AI platforms appear to have stalled at checkout. Google has been actively developing its own AI commerce standard, but hasn't uttered a word about the agentic travel booking tool that may rely on it, since its initial tease in 2025. ChatGPT, meanwhile, has faced challenges converting conversational interest into completed bookings.

Amazon's approach leverages its massive installed base: 180 million Prime members worldwide, many of whom already trust the platform with payment information. By integrating travel booking directly into Alexa+ conversations, the company aims to reduce friction and increase conversion rates.

The Model Context Protocol allows travel companies to plug their inventory directly into Alexa+'s AI engine, enabling real-time availability and pricing. For travelers, this means they can ask for a flight, hotel, or ride and complete the transaction without leaving the chat interface.

Early partners like Priceline and Viator bring extensive travel inventory, while Virgin Atlantic and Brightline offer specific transportation options. Lyft and Greyhound cover ground transportation, creating a multi-modal booking experience.

However, questions remain about data ownership and privacy. Travel companies are wary of ceding customer relationships to Amazon, which could use the data to refine its own travel offerings or target ads. The new wallet feature, while convenient, may further entrench Amazon's role as the intermediary.

Despite these concerns, the potential is significant. If Alexa+ can successfully convert conversational queries into bookings, it could reshape how travelers plan and purchase trips. The test with Priceline and other partners later this year will be closely watched by the industry.

For now, Amazon is positioning Alexa+ as a platform that can do what other AI assistants have failed to do: close the sale. With MCP support and a built-in wallet, the company is betting that convenience will win over both travelers and travel companies.
