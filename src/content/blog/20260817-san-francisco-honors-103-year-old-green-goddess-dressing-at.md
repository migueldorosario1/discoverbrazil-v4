Forget sourdough for a moment. One of America's most famous dressings was born in a San Francisco luxury hotel — and on August 22, the Palace is putting its Green Goddess back in the spotlight.

San Francisco has officially proclaimed August 23 Green Goddess Day, celebrating a culinary creation born not in a food laboratory or trendy California restaurant, but inside one of the city's grandest landmarks: The Palace Hotel.

And the Palace is celebrating. On Saturday, August 22, one day before the official proclamation date, the historic hotel will serve a special Green Goddess Three Ways lunch at its iconic Pied Piper Bar & Lounge.

For foodies, San Francisco locals, visitors, gourmet travelers — and tour operators searching for the next authentic culinary experience to put into a California program — this is a delicious piece of living history.

## Before Green Goddess Went Global, It Was San Francisco

The story starts in 1923. Palace Hotel Chef Philip Roemer created Green Goddess dressing for actor George Arliss, star of the hit play 'The Green Goddess.' A century before 'going viral' became part of our vocabulary, this San Francisco creation did essentially that.

Green Goddess escaped the Palace kitchen, spread across America and became one of those recipes that generations of chefs and home cooks would reinterpret again and again. But there is only one birthplace: San Francisco's Palace Hotel. That makes Green Goddess more than something to pour over lettuce. It is edible San Francisco history.

## Three Ways to Meet the Goddess

For one lunch service, the Palace is giving its famous creation the star treatment with Green Goddess Three Ways at Pied Piper Bar & Lounge. The menu includes:

- Signature Green Goddess Harvest & West Coast Crab Salad
- Green Goddess Smoothie Shooter
- Green Goddess Dip with Crudité

West Coast crab and Green Goddess? That alone may be worth putting August 22 on a San Francisco food lover's calendar. But the real attraction is the story surrounding the plate. You aren't simply eating another interpretation of a fashionable herb dressing. You're tasting a dish in the very hotel where its story began 103 years ago. Try finding that on an ordinary sightseeing tour.

## The Pied Piper Is No Ordinary Hotel Bar

Then there is the location. Pied Piper Bar & Lounge is one of those San Francisco places that visitors could easily walk past without realizing what is waiting inside. Located in the Palace Hotel at 2 New Montgomery Street, the Pied Piper has been attracting San Franciscans and international visitors for more than a century.

Its rich wood interior, mosaic tile floor, and classic atmosphere already make it a destination. But look around, and you'll discover the extraordinary Maxfield Parrish Pied Piper mural that gives the bar its name. A historic hotel. A famous work of art. Cocktails. California cuisine. And now the hometown celebration of a culinary icon created under the same roof. This isn't just lunch. It's a San Francisco story you can eat.

## Culinary Tour Operators, Take Note

San Francisco has never had difficulty selling itself as a food destination. Chinatown. North Beach. Dungeness crab. Sourdough. Farmers markets. California wine. Michelin-starred restaurants. Neighborhood kitchens representing cuisines from around the world.

But sophisticated culinary travelers increasingly want something beyond another restaurant reservation. They want stories. They want to know what was invented where, who ate it, why it mattered and whether they can experience it today. Green Goddess delivers exactly that.

For tour operators, DMCs and travel advisors designing gourmet itineraries, the Palace Hotel offers an unusually marketable combination of food, history, architecture, art and luxury hospitality in one downtown location. A Green Goddess experience can be paired with Palace history, the hotel's famous Garden Court, the Pied Piper and further exploration of San Francisco's downtown culinary scene. That's not simply lunch. That's a sellable culinary-tourism experience.

## San Francisco Should Own the Green Goddess Story

Here's the bigger question: Why isn't Green Goddess already as closely associated with San Francisco as sourdough? The city has something destination marketers spend fortunes trying to manufacture: an authentic culinary story with a recognizable name, a documented historic setting and a recipe that survived for more than a century.

No branding agency had to invent it. Chef Philip Roemer already did the work in 1923. Now San Francisco has officially given the Goddess her day. August 23 is Green Goddess Day. And on August 22, the Palace Hotel is inviting San Francisco to taste the history that started it all.

## 103 Years Old — and Still Deliciously Relevant

Food trends come and go at astonishing speed. Green Goddess stayed. Perhaps that's because its combination of herbs, freshness and unmistakably Californian personality feels remarkably contemporary for something created when Calvin Coolidge was president. Or perhaps great recipes simply don't need an expiration date.

Either way, San Francisco now has an excellent reason to reclaim one of its tastiest contributions to American culinary history. So when someone asks what they should eat on their next trip to San Francisco, don't stop at sourdough, crab or a Mission burrito. Tell them to meet the Goddess where she was born.
