Google Maps is turning its location-based platform into a smarter travel companion. The five-month-old Ask Maps feature now handles conversational hotel searches, letting travelers describe what they want in plain language and get real pricing and availability back — not estimates.

According to skift.com, the tool combines proximity, routes, nearby businesses, and hotel criteria in a single query. You can ask for a hotel near a specific gym or restaurant, for example, and Maps will factor in the vibe, price, ratings, and how close it is to the places you care about.

A new opt-in Personal Intelligence integration takes this further. By connecting your Gmail, Ask Maps can pull in existing reservations — flights, hotels, dinners — and layer that personal itinerary context onto the geographic search. The result: suggestions like how to spend a few hours near your hotel before a flight, based on what you already have booked.

For now, hotel functionality stops at discovery and comparison. Travelers cannot book directly through the chat yet. But Google has quietly started a limited U.S. test of agentic hotel booking, working with partners such as Amadeus and direct suppliers, according to skift.com.

The infrastructure for in-chat transactions is already live for food ordering. Through Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP), with partners like Square and Toast, users can complete food orders without leaving the conversation. That signals the intent to eventually enable end-to-end hotel booking at Maps' scale.

And the scale is significant. Maps now surpasses 2 billion monthly users, according to skift.com. The key open question is whether Google will tie its discovery, personal context, and transaction capabilities together for hotels — making location, surroundings, and 'vibe' increasingly decisive at the moment of booking.

For travelers heading to Brazil, this could change how you plan your stay. Instead of juggling multiple apps, you might soon ask Maps for a pousada in Paraty with good breakfast and a short walk to the historic center, and get live options that match your itinerary.

The feature is still evolving, but the direction is clear: search is becoming a conversation, and your travel plans are becoming part of the map. As Google continues to test agentic booking, the line between planning and booking is likely to blur — starting with the places you stay.
