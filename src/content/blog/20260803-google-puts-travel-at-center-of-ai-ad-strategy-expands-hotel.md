Google is betting big on travel to prove its artificial intelligence strategy can generate advertising revenue. During its latest earnings call, the company highlighted travel as a flagship example of how AI can match paid offers to user intent.

According to Skift, travel searches contain rich, structured signals — dates, budgets, destinations, and amenities — that give Google's Gemini-powered AI tools stronger data to surface relevant ads. This makes the category ideal for testing new monetization models.

Google is operationalizing this through its Direct Offers pilot, which will soon surface promotions from IHG Hotels & Resorts during AI-assisted trip planning. IHG joins earlier partners Booking Holdings and Expedia in the program.

At the same time, Google is building booking infrastructure around its AI environment. The company is expanding the Universal Commerce Protocol into hotel booking, working toward letting travelers complete flight and hotel reservations directly within AI Mode — which now surpasses 1 billion monthly active users.

Travel companies are hedging their bets across multiple AI platforms rather than committing to one. IHG, for example, also distributes inventory via a ChatGPT app that lets users search over 7,000 hotels. Booking Holdings powers agentic reservations on OpenTable.

While Google declined to disclose travel-specific metrics or click-through rates, it signaled clear intent to consolidate ads, booking tools, and paid placements inside the same conversational interface where travelers plan their trips.

For travelers, this means future trip planning could become more seamless — but also more commercial. As Google weaves paid offers into AI conversations, the line between helpful planning and advertising may blur.

The move underscores how travel remains a critical testbed for AI monetization, given its complex decision-making and high transaction value. Whether travelers embrace booking through chatbots remains to be seen, but Google is clearly betting they will.
