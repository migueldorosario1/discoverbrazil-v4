Experienced travelers and winter sports enthusiasts are buzzing with excitement as Chile's ski resorts experience a remarkable recovery following a multi-year drought. Valle Nevado, the largest ski area in the Southern Hemisphere, has reported a substantial 315 cm of total snow at an elevation of 3,000 m as of early July 2026, a stark contrast to recent years' underwhelming snowpacks, according to riotimesonline.com.

*A Winter Wonderland Returns*
The 2026 winter season welcomed early storms that dusted the high-altitude resorts east of Santiago, bringing back a landscape that had become increasingly brown and barren. Despite a temporary lull in snowfall in late July with no fresh accumulation, the substantial base established by early accumulations has sustained resort operations.

*Neighborhood Resorts Benefit*
Neighboring resort La Parva, favored by affluent Chilean families and second-home owners, also capitalized on the early snow. Even though daily snow reports later showed zero fresh accumulation, the base held firm, supported by earlier storms. Independent snow-tracking services like Snowsure.ai and J2Ski reported modest depths at La Parva with readings of 10 centimeters on the mountain and a 5-centimeter base in some areas.

*Economic Boom and International Appeal*
International visitors are the economic backbone of Chile's ski industry. Before the drought, approximately 60% of Valle Nevado's guests were from abroad, with Brazilians being the largest foreign group. North Americans and Europeans seeking off-season skiing in July and August are another significant segment. The partial reopening of the resort signaled to the global audience that Chile's ski scene is back on the map.

*Economic Impact and Revival*
A full ski season at Chile's interconnected resorts near Santiago can generate over US$150 million in direct and indirect revenue. This figure accounts for lift-ticket sales, hotel bookings, restaurant revenue, equipment rentals, and transportation services. The drought years had significantly reduced these numbers, with hotels reporting occupancy rates below 30% during peak ski weeks. The 2026 rebound is reviving a crucial employment engine for the area.

*Looking Ahead*
The Southern Hemisphere ski season continues through late September, with August historically delivering the deepest snowpack. Long-range forecasts suggest that a La Niña pattern could bring above-average precipitation to the central Chilean Andes, although meteorologists advise caution with such predictions. Valle Nevado's season-pass sales indicate a surge in confidence, with early-bird pricing selling faster than in 2025.

For now, the images of white ridges above Santiago have restored a sense of normalcy, and skiers are enjoying the slopes that were barren just a year ago. The comeback might not be complete, but with 32 centimeters of snow and the promise of more, the foundation for a vibrant ski season is the strongest in half a decade.
