A collection of 13 landmark buildings designed by pioneering modernist architects Aino, Elissa and Alvar Aalto has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, recognizing the global significance of their human-centered approach to architecture and design, according to eturbonews.com.

The newly designated Aalto Works celebrates buildings that demonstrate how thoughtful design can enhance wellbeing, equality and community life while continuing to influence architecture around the world.

Helsinki serves as the gateway to the UNESCO site, with five of the 13 buildings located in the Finnish capital — the largest concentration within the designation. Highlights include the Aalto House, Studio Aalto, Finlandia Hall, the House of Culture and the National Pensions Institute (KELA).

City leaders say the recognition strengthens Helsinki's position as a world-class destination for architecture, offering visitors the chance to experience the Aaltos' enduring legacy through iconic public spaces, cultural venues and even overnight stays in the recently restored Finlandia Hall.

For travelers interested in design and architecture, Helsinki now offers a unique opportunity to explore the Aalto masterpieces up close. The five sites are easily accessible within the city, making it possible to visit them all in a day or two. Finlandia Hall, in particular, has been restored and now hosts events and even overnight accommodations, allowing guests to sleep inside a UNESCO-listed building.

The Aalto Works designation is a testament to Finland's rich architectural heritage and its commitment to preserving modernist masterpieces. Whether you are an architecture enthusiast or a curious traveler, Helsinki's Aalto sites provide a profound insight into how design can shape human experience.
