Delta Air Lines is turning heads with a rare award sale for its upcoming Riyadh route. Members of its loyalty program can book one-way flights from Atlanta to Riyadh for as little as 15,000 miles on select dates, according to Skift.

The redemption roughly equals $220 in cash, including an $8 fee, based on The Points Guy awards calculator. For customers holding one of Delta's co-branded credit cards, the price drops further to 12,700 miles, translating to about $187 with fees.

This is notably cheap for a 13-hour international flight. While airlines routinely run award promotions, Delta's Riyadh redemptions stand out as particularly low, signaling a strong push to fill seats ahead of the October 23 launch.

Delta is betting on a mix of business and leisure travel to Saudi Arabia. The carrier faces a market where other airlines have struggled to establish a foothold, making this aggressive pricing a strategic move to attract early demand.

The Atlanta-Riyadh service marks Delta's entry into a growing Gulf market, with Saudi Arabia investing heavily in tourism and business connectivity. For travelers, the sale offers a budget-friendly way to explore a destination that is opening up to international visitors.

Award availability is limited to select dates and basic economy fares, so flexibility is key. Delta's co-branded cardholders get an extra edge, with a steeper discount that makes the redemption even more attractive.

This sale is a clear signal of Delta's commitment to making the route work. By pricing awards so competitively, the airline is not only generating buzz but also testing the waters for sustained demand on a route that others have found challenging.

For now, the offer gives savvy travelers a rare chance to book a long-haul flight to the Middle East at a fraction of the usual cost. Whether this strategy pays off will depend on how quickly the seats fill and whether the route can attract repeat business.

Delta's move also reflects broader trends in airline loyalty programs, where carriers use miles as a flexible tool to stimulate demand on new routes. The Riyadh sale is a textbook example of using award inventory to build early momentum.

Travelers interested in the deal should act fast, as award seats at these levels tend to disappear quickly. The October 23 launch is just around the corner, and Delta is clearly eager to start the route with a full cabin.

As the aviation industry watches, Delta's Riyadh experiment could offer lessons for other carriers eyeing the Saudi market. With low award prices and a dual focus on business and leisure, the airline is positioning itself for a strong debut.

For now, the message is simple: Delta wants to fly you to Riyadh, and it is willing to make the miles work in your favor.
