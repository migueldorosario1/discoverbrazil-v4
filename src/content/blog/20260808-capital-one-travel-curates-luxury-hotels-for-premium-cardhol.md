Credit card travel programs are reshaping how travelers discover and book trips. According to Skift Research, these programs now rival traditional airline and hotel loyalty programs in perceived value. In the U.S., 34% of surveyed travelers said credit card programs offer the most rewarding benefits, compared with 22% for hotel programs and 21% for airlines.

This shift has led leading travel credit cards to build curated hotel programs that go beyond points. For premium cardholders, value now comes from access, benefits, guidance, and a more thoughtful way to choose where to stay.

Capital One Travel has applied this thinking to its Premier and Lifestyle Collections, launched in 2023 as an extension of its premium travel strategy. These collections give eligible cardholders access to handpicked hotels, resorts, and vacation rentals, with perks like experience credits and room upgrades.

The Premier Collection focuses on luxury properties with elevated service and bespoke amenities. The Lifestyle Collection emphasizes design-forward, locally rooted stays with a distinctive sense of place. Since launch, the collections have expanded to more than 1,400 properties.

'Luxury has become an individualized definition, and our role is to curate experiences that help travelers discover what luxury means to them,' said Phillip LaRue, head of luxury travel at Capital One. 'By highlighting diverse properties, our curated portfolio delivers a powerful sense of discovery to cardholders.'

Curation gives structure to a category that has become increasingly personal. The collections help cardholders move from a wide field of options to a focused set of stays selected for service, character, and relevance to the trip.

'Today's travelers are increasingly seeking experiences that feel authentic, memorable, and tailored to their interests rather than simply choosing the most expensive option available,' LaRue said. 'Our Premier and Lifestyle Collections act as an expert travel curator when planning trips.'

The portfolios include independent and lifestyle properties, as well as partnerships with groups such as Small Luxury Hotels of the World and The Leading Hotels of the World. They also feature established luxury brands like Belmond, Peninsula, and Six Senses, plus a growing set of Accor brands, including Fairmont and Raffles.

The emphasis is less on the length of the list than on its range. In Puerto Rico, for example, the Premier Collection includes Fairmont El San Juan, a heritage luxury property, while the Lifestyle Collection includes Hotel El Convento, an independent boutique hotel in one of San Juan's most historic buildings. Both are elevated stays, but each offers a different expression of the destination.

Behind the collections is a selection process that combines customer insight, partner evaluation, and ongoing quality control. The goal is not to apply a single definition of luxury across every market, but to identify properties that deliver strong hospitality while adding something distinctive.

'Our curation process combines data-driven analysis with a highly selective qualitative review,' said Hya Valdez, head of luxury travel business development and strategic partnerships at Capital One Travel. 'We spend significant time understanding a property's unique story and what distinguishes it within its market while evaluating whether it aligns with the type of experiences our cardholders are seeking.'

Capital One Travel considers guest satisfaction, service quality, amenities, location, operational consistency, and overall guest experience. Once a property joins the portfolio, the team continues to monitor performance and work with partners to ensure standards are met.

The collections have scaled quickly, but the company frames that growth as intentional rather than volume-driven. As more hotels compete for premium travelers, curated distribution can create new discovery pathways for properties with strong service models and a defined sense of place.

'The growth has been driven by a combination of strong traveler demand, positive partner engagement, and a deliberate strategy to expand thoughtfully across destinations and travel styles,' LaRue said.

For hotel partners, the distinction is both commercial and editorial. A property does not only need to be bookable; it needs to be understandable within the context of the trip a traveler wants to take. A glamping retreat, for example, isn't competing directly with a five-star city hotel. Each serves a different travel moment, and curation helps travelers find the property that best matches their experience.

Lifestyle-driven hotels have become a bigger part of the premium travel landscape as travelers seek stronger design and deeper local context. Skift Advisory research found that travelers are willing to pay a 40% premium for unique, distinctive local experiences over standard accommodation quality.

For Capital One Travel, the Lifestyle Collection gives cardholders another way into a destination, shaped less by traditional luxury cues and more by culture, design, and neighborhood context.

'Independent and distinctive properties often provide the type of authentic, locally connected experiences that many travelers are increasingly seeking,' Valdez said. 'These hotels frequently have a strong sense of place, unique design perspectives, and highly personalized service models that create memorable stays.'

Valdez pointed to brands such as Bunkhouse Hotels and Ash Hotels as examples of properties 'embedding travelers directly into the neighborhood fabric by integrating local-favorite culinary, art, and music as part of the guest experience.'

As traveler expectations become more personal, destination strategy is becoming part of how Capital One Travel helps cardholders discover what luxury means to them. In markets with strong demand, the goal is to offer a range of stays that can support different trip occasions, from established resorts to boutique hideaways.
