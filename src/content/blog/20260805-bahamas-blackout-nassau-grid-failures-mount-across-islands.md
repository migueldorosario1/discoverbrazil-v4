A severe Bahamas blackout plunged the entire island of New Providence into darkness on July 30, 2026, after an electrical fire at the Blue Hills Power Station caused a transformer to explode. The incident was the most dramatic in a series of island-wide power failures across the archipelago, exposing vulnerabilities in the nation's electrical grid and disrupting life for residents and tourists alike.

The complete loss of power supply across New Providence, the nation's most populous island, was triggered by a suspected underground cable fault. According to a report from Tribune242, this fault caused the explosion of a 15MVA transformer at the Blue Hills Power Station. Bahamas Power and Light (BPL) crews responded to the emergency, and by 7:18 a.m., the utility confirmed that electricity had been restored to approximately 50% of consumers. The staged restoration brought power back to areas including Bonita Bay, Winton, Yellow Elder, Stapleton Gardens, Paradise Island, East Street South, South Beach Estates, Pinewood, Marshall Road, Bamboo Town, Fox Hill Road, Gladstone Road, Firetrail Road, Carmichael Road, and Sears Road. The final full-restoration time for the entire island was not immediately available in public reports.

The government responded to the New Providence crisis by stating that the Prime Minister's office was working directly with BPL to manage the situation. Officials reported that all engines were back up and running and that technical teams were systematically bringing remaining areas online as the system was carefully calibrated. The incident highlighted the fragility of the aging power infrastructure, with separate 2026 reports noting that outages had previously affected critical facilities like Princess Margaret Hospital and forced some businesses to close, implying significant economic and public service disruptions beyond the immediate loss of electricity.

A separate island-wide Bahamas blackout struck Cat Island on June 6, 2026, with a direct and quantifiable impact on tourism. BPL reported that a fire involving the Cat Island 1 (CI 1) engine began at about 8:30 p.m. after the turbocharger failed and leaked oil onto the exhaust manifold. The resulting blaze caused a total power loss across the island. BPL explicitly stated that the outage affected the closing night of the Rake & Scrape Festival, a significant cultural event, leading to its cancellation. Full power was not restored until 6:14 p.m. on June 7, 2026, meaning residents and visitors were without electricity for approximately 21 hours and 46 minutes.

The incidents in New Providence and Cat Island were not isolated. A pattern of widespread power failures affected multiple Bahamian islands throughout 2026. On March 30, BPL reported successive outages across Eleuthera caused by system faults, with severe weather including lightning and a pole fire in South Eleuthera contributing to the problems. In June, Eyewitness News reported an outage on Exuma affecting about 1,500 customers from Hermitage to Williams Town, alongside a smaller outage for about 40 customers in Farmer's Cay and a brief disruption for roughly 190 customers on Mayaguana.

The strain on the electrical grid continued into July. Tribune242 reported weekend outages across both Abaco and New Providence, with parts of Abaco experiencing an 18-hour power loss. The causes cited for these disruptions included lightning, cable faults, damaged equipment, transformer problems, and heavy electricity demand. In a separate and unusual incident on Grand Bahama, the Grand Bahama Power Company was forced to conduct an emergency shutdown after a bomb threat, cutting power across large parts of that island for hours. While no quantified tourism losses in US dollars are available for these events, the repeated disruptions to businesses, hospitals, and cultural festivals paint a clear picture of a utility struggling to maintain reliable service.

For travelers planning a trip to the Bahamas, these outages serve as a reminder to check with local authorities and accommodations about backup power availability, especially during peak seasons. The Rake & Scrape Festival cancellation on Cat Island underscores how infrastructure failures can directly impact cultural tourism. As the archipelago works to modernize its grid, visitors should stay flexible and informed.
